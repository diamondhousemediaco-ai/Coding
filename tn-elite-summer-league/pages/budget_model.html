No cash prizes — winners take hardware, a banner in the building, champion gear, and an automatic invite back next year. Officiating stays college-quality but tournament-sized: 2-man crews pulled from the same D2 roster (two-person college mechanics is a real system, not a downgrade — and still miles above any AAU run), with 3-man crews for semifinals and finals. That makes tournaments genuinely profitable: ~$20K net at 32 teams, ~$33K at 40. Every visiting team is next year's draft pipeline, hotel room-nights, and vendor traffic. Option B totals assume one confirmed 40-team event; the optional second adds another ~$33K net.

Two rules that keep Option B clean

1 · No cash prizes, period. Champions take trophies, belts, a banner in the building, champion gear, and an automatic invite back — never money. That removes every amateur-status question before it's asked, and it prices the tournament honestly: teams are buying exposure and competition, not chasing a purse. 2 · Tournament weekends get NCAA event certification just like league marquee weekends, so the live-period college-coach draw is legal and loud. Also fold in: entry discounts for teams that book through league housing (stay-to-play) — room-night leverage starts Year 1.
