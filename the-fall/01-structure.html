The spine

Want it → get it → lose it → be it.

Bill wants to be known. He gets known — as a punchline, under a name that isn't even his. The fall gives him everything and shows him it's nothing. The second fall takes it all back and hands him himself. Known is what he wanted. Seen is what he needed, and she was behind the counter in frame one. It resolves on him marrying her — with nothing, in a small apartment, exactly where he started.

Act One ends with
THE FIRST FALL

He wipes out on the one-wheel and the internet is filming. It gives him money, fame, and a fake name. The jackpot he could never hit by trying, hit by failing.

The machine underneath both
THE ONE-WHEEL

It's the same accident twice. Early on, a one-wheel spill wrecks his one real date — private, humiliating, costs him nothing but the night. Later, the identical wipeout happens with two kids filming, and it hands him everything. Nothing about Bill changed. Only who was watching. That's the movie's argument in one prop.

Act Three opens with
THE SECOND FALL

Stocks to zero, the building burns, a car goes up. It takes back everything the first fall gave him — and leaves the only thing he actually needed.

Before Stage 2

Six things to decide

The ending is locked — he marries the barista. These are the open choices that change actual scenes, so they're worth calling before we break them out.

  1. Bill's job. What does he do in act one? It sets the boss, the coffee run, and what he has to lose. A job with a uniform is funnier — and it makes the fame contrast land.
  2. The life-insurance scheme. Is he selling policies door to door — the least safe man alive pitching peace of mind, wrecking every living room he enters — or buying them hoping for a payout? Selling is funnier and safer; buying is darker.
  3. Does he still ride the one-wheel at the end? It wrecked his date, then made him famous, then the fame ate itself. Him riding it in the final scene — still wobbling, no audience, happy — would be the whole movie in one image.
  4. Does she get a name? Strong option: she's "the barista" to us through most of act one because Bill has never asked. Him finally asking is its own scene.
  5. Do the twins come back? Best payoff: they run into him at the end and don't recognize him — the two people who made him famous can't pick him out of a crowd.
  6. Nashville — committing? The twins' line says yes. It gives us a real city, a music-money world the rapper comes from, and specific texture instead of Anytown.
  7. The wedding — who's there? Since it's the resolution, the guest list is the theme. A tiny wedding with almost nobody says he stopped needing a crowd. Worth deciding now because it's the last image before the apartment.
