HOW TO USE THIS

WHAT THE MOVIE IS ABOUT

Being KNOWN versus being SEEN.

He wants a million strangers to love him. He gets it — as a punchline,
under a name that isn't even his. The one person who ever actually saw
him has been there since they were kids.

Underneath that: he can't accept love he didn't earn. That's why he
can't see her, and why he needs strangers instead.

TONE

Starts broad and funny. Gets less funny and more serious as the money
piles up — you're supposed to feel it. Then SHE brings the funny back,
because the clumsiness is what makes him him.

The rule for every disaster in the script:

  CLUMSINESS — lovable, never actually costs him anything, and it's
  what she falls for. (The coffee, the wipeout, the carnival prize.)

  REACHING — the real flaw, and the ONLY thing that ever takes
  something from him. (The pitch on the date, buying the girl,
  snapping at her.)

Every disaster should sort into one of those two.

14. "I QUIT"
----------------------------------------------------------------
Cars first. He pulls up outside the office and lays on the horn —

15. THE SIGNING
----------------------------------------------------------------
*** HE DOESN'T BUY ANY OF IT. HE LEASES IT. ***

The mansion is rented or barely mortgaged. The Lamborghinis and
Ferraris are leased. The yacht is chartered by the day. The clothes
and parties are on credit. He signs everything without reading a word,
because reading would slow down the part where he gets to feel rich.

This does three things: it makes the money believable, it starts a
clock the audience can watch, and it says the whole theme in one word
— HE IS RENTING A LIFE.

35. THE BOARDROOM               *** THE CLIMAX. HIS RACE. ***
----------------------------------------------------------------
He blows it up. AND HE IS TERRIBLE AT IT — sweating, dropping pages,
losing his place, the projector fighting him.

He carries in THE STACK. Nineteen years of the boss's work in Bill's
own handwriting. It isn't just evidence, it's proof of who actually
ran that company.

And he is completely, devastatingly right.

*** For the first time in the movie he does something that costs him
everything and gains him nothing. NO CAMERA. NO CROWD. NO UPSIDE.
He performed for millions and got nothing real. He does one honest
thing for nobody, and THAT is what finally gets him seen. ***

He walks out with no job and it's the best he has ever felt.

PART FIVE — WHAT'S STILL OPEN
================================================================

Anything you don't decide, the writer decides. That's the only test
that matters — not "am I allowed to leave this blank," but "am I okay
with somebody else answering it?"

  [ ] What the barista wants for herself
  [ ] Her name

  [ ] What the boss's fraud actually is
