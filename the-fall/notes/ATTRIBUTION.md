- **"Nothing ever goes my way"**
- He wants to be rich, famous, known, popular and **loved by everybody**
- **He loves validation** — the money is only so people will see and like him,
  and he doesn't realize that's the problem

  himself → finds who he really is and the love of his life

# MINE — STRUCTURE, CONNECTIONS, AND CRAFT

Most of these are organizing or connecting *your* material rather than inventing
story.

## Structural
- The three-act / eleven-stage breakdown and the beat ordering
- **The ending order: he marries her broke, and the CEO offer comes two years
  later** — so status isn't what makes him happy
- **The boardroom as the climax** rather than the promotion, so he acts
- Making the birthday party and the one-year-anniversary-of-the-fall party **the
  same brand-sponsored event** — which explains its size and why nobody knows him
- **Act Two-B as a ladder** where he dismisses all four real people one at a time
- Capping Act Three at three moves so it doesn't become a procedural

## Rules and readings
- **"Two kinds of disaster"** — clumsiness never costs him anything and is what
  she loves; reaching is the real flaw and the only thing that ever takes
  something from him
- **He can't accept love he didn't earn** — why he can't see her, and why he needs
  strangers *(prompted by your "should he have a mom?")*
- **He's blind to the gold digger on purpose**, because seeing her means admitting
  the money didn't make him lovable
- **He becomes the thing that ruined his life** — he overlooks Clyde exactly as he
  was overlooked. The worst thing, refined from your principle
- **The boss picked Bill's floor because nobody there matters** — overlooked
  people are the safest to frame
- **Act Two is an addiction arc**, not a greed arc *(from your dopamine note)*
- The tone curve as a measurable shape

## Devices, plants and payoffs
- **Leasing everything instead of buying it** — fixes the math, creates the clock,
  and says "he rented a life"
- **He never owned anything** — the beat before the crash out
- **Cars repossessed one at a time; the mansion as the fixed cost that kills him**
- **The stack of paper** as a plant that pays off in the boardroom
- **The company photo with his face hidden**, paying off as the final image
- **The ding as a sound motif — and the silence when the fame dies**
- **The catchphrase failing him** at the crash out
- **The two stock crashes shot identically**, so the second one lands
- **The dancing-alone shot as the thesis image**, and as the bookend to the
  crash out *(the dancing is yours)*
- **The same store three times** — they look down on him, they fawn, they hand the
  card back
- **Crossing the street** from Payless to the designer store, and back *(Payless
  is yours)*
- **The costume thread** — the hoodie, the designer fit, and nothing at the end
- **The MLM boxes** that never leave the apartment
- **The failed viral attempts** — he tried to go viral on purpose
- **The rear-end scam that fails because he celebrates**
- **The handwriting** as the proof
- **His invisibility as how he gets onto the top floor**
- **The kids made the boardroom deck**, which is why it's terrible
