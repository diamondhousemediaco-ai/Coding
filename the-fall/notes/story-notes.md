# THE FALL — Story Notes (everything on one sheet)

Raw thinking, organized. This is the source of truth for the idea; the HTML pages are
built from this. Nothing here is locked **except the ending** (see below).

> Source was dictated, so the original notes had transcription typos. Those are
> normalized here — read for intent, not for literal wording. Dialogue in quotes is
> kept close to how it was said, because the rhythm is the point.

---

## The one-liner

A walking disaster in his 40s–50s finally gets everything he ever wanted — rich,
famous, known — by accident, discovers it's empty, loses all of it, and ends up back
where he started with the one person who actually saw him.

## Theme

**KNOWN vs. SEEN.**

He wants to be known. He gets known — as the klutz, the punchline, "the fall." The
crowd doesn't even have his name right. The only person who ever *saw* him was the
barista, and she was there in the first frame.

## The man

- Name: **Bill**
- Age: **late 40s / 50s** — this is load-bearing. The comedy is a middle-aged man
  living out a rich teenager's fantasy.
- Motto: **"Nothing ever goes my way."**
- Good-hearted, earnest, and catastrophically clumsy. Makes a *ton* of mistakes.
- Wants: to be rich, famous, known, popular, and **loved / validated by everybody** —
  ideally by tomorrow.
- Needs: to be seen for who he is, and to be okay with that man.
- The trick: the clumsiness isn't a flaw to fix. **It's him.** The world laughs *at*
  it. She laughs *with* it. That's the whole movie.

## The get-rich schemes (before the fall)

- Lottery tickets — buys them by the fistful
- Stock market — buys in, it immediately craters
- Life insurance — *(open question: selling policies door to door, or buying one
  hoping for a payout? Selling is funnier and safer: the least safe man alive
  pitching peace of mind)*

All of it is chasing the same thing: **validation.** He's trying to buy proof that
he counts.

## Mess-ups / set pieces in hand

- Spills coffee on himself at his job
- Boss yelling at him — default volume is a shout
- Dating apps, none of it goes anywhere
- One real date — wrecked by something stupid. *(Open: what exactly. It can't be an
  accident — the one-wheel is the movie's only accident.)*
- **Fortnite:** playing with a squad of young kids, he's the last one alive and dies
  to *fall damage*. Narration over it: *"yeah, those are actually some of the
  closest friends I've had in a long time."* Cut back to: *"come on, come on Tre,
  don't do this to me. Jacob — Jacob — Jacob, come on."* / *"sorry man, I gotta go."*

## The inciting incident — THE FALL

Two twins in flat-bills turned to the side. Bill goes by on his one-wheel.

> "Yo, look at this guy on the one-wheel vibing. This is Nashville baby. Record it."
> — *(he falls)* —
> "OHHHHHH."
> "No way you got that bro."
> "Should I post it? I kinda feel bad."
> "Definitely post it."

Overnight, Bill blows up. Influencers everywhere. Brand deals, commercials, morning
news. Completely explodes. **The jackpot he could never hit by trying, he hit by
failing.**

## The fame stretch

- Buys the big house, buys all the cars
- Throws parties. At first they're hilarious — jumping in the pool, going crazy
- The parties get **bigger and bigger** and Bill gets **less and less**
- **The "Bob" birthday:** hires a rapper for his own birthday party. Rapper yells
  *"Big happy birthday to our guy BOB!"* — not his name — and the whole crowd goes
  *"woooo, let's go Bob, happy birthday!"* A thousand strangers screaming a name
  that isn't his. Nobody there actually knows who he is.
- With all the money, cars, fame, hype — he's still empty. By the end he realizes
  it isn't for him, and he's okay with that.

## The second fall (losing it)

- Blows all his money
- Loses it in stocks
- Buys real estate, it burns down
- One of his cars gets blown up
- The same stupid stuff that always happened to him keeps happening — just
  expensive now

## Her

- **The barista** at the coffee shop he's gone to for years. The girl of his dreams.
- She should be on screen **from the first scene** — she's the throughline, not a
  third-act love interest.
- Once he's rich he tries to win her with money and stuff. She says she doesn't
  care about that. He genuinely likes her, so he has to win her over by just
  being himself.
- **The carnival / amusement park date:** he tries to win her a prize and completely
  fails, and he's upset at himself — and she thinks it's the cutest, funniest thing
  ever.
- **The fight:** he gets upset at her and says *"nobody likes me for who I really
  am."* She tears up and tells him **she does** — and he somehow blows it.
- She's the one who brings the funniness back out of him. The clumsiness is what
  makes him him, and she's the only one who ever knew that.

## The ending — LOCKED

**The resolution is that he marries the barista.**

He wins her with nothing but himself, they get married, and they move into a small
apartment. The movie ends where it started — but he's okay with himself, and he has
the love of his life. He never got known the way he wanted. He got something better.

Open: who's actually at the wedding. Since it's the resolution, the guest list *is*
the theme — a tiny wedding with almost nobody says he stopped needing a crowd.

### The final image — the bike

**Leaning:** in the small apartment, **she buys him a bike.** A bike has *two*
wheels — the joke is that she's quietly looking after him, affectionate instead of
mocking. He's touched. He gets on it. He immediately eats it. She loses it laughing,
and **he laughs too.**

Why it's the right ending: it's the first time in the movie he laughs at his own
fall. The first fall was filmed by strangers laughing *at* him and it cost him
everything. This one has an audience of one laughing *with* him and it costs him
nothing. **Same man, same clumsiness — different witness.** The whole theme lands
as a physical gag with no dialogue.

Variants:
- **Training wheels on the bike** — funnier reveal, bigger laugh, but makes her more
  teasing and him more childlike, which slightly undercuts "she sees the man"
- **He still rides the one-wheel** — simplest and most defiant, but it's *his* choice
  instead of *her* gesture, so it closes his arc and not theirs

## Tone rule (important)

The beginning is **funny**. As he gets more money and more famous it gets **less
funny and more serious** — you're supposed to *feel* it. Then **she** brings the
funny back, because that clumsiness is what makes him him.

So the laugh count is a curve, not a line: high → hollow → warm.

## Motifs to plant and pay off

- **The one-wheel** — there is exactly **one accident** in the movie and this is it.
  Act one has plenty of small daily disasters, but only one real accident. Keeping it
  singular is what makes it *the* fall instead of one more pratfall. Open: does he
  still ride it at the end?
- **"The Fall"** — the literal wipeout · the fall from grace · Fortnite *fall damage*
- **"Bob," not Bill** — known but not seen. Pays off at the end when she says his
  real name and means it.
- **"Nothing ever goes my way"** — said as a complaint in act one; said as a joke,
  happily, at the end.
- **The coffee shop** — first scene and last scene.

## Open questions

   asks? (Him asking is its own scene.)
4. Do the twins come back at the end? (They could be the ones who don't recognize

5. Is it Nashville for real? The twins' line suggests yes — worth committing to.
6. Who's at the wedding?
7. What exactly wrecks the date? (Small, stupid, his fault — and not an accident.)
8. Final image — the bike, the bike with training wheels, or the one-wheel?
   (Leaning: the bike. See "The final image" above.)
