The Stage & The Stream

Built For Broadcast

"The highest stage before college" is a production claim, not a slogan. Every game streams from day one, and there's a deliberate ladder from our own channels to ESPN. Here's where games live, what real production costs, and how to make every night read big-league on camera.

LEVEL 1 · YEAR 1

Own Channels

League YouTube for full games + TikTok/IG/YT Shorts highlight engine. This is OTE's actual model — 23M views on opening weekend, all owned audience and data. Free distribution, and sponsors buy the clips.

LEVEL 2 · YEAR 1

League Pass

Summer pass ($10–15/mo) or per-season pass on our site. Parents, relatives, and college staffs subscribe — BallerTV built an entire business on exactly this. Revenue, plus emails of every fan.

LEVEL 3 · YEAR 2

Local TV

Nashville independent stations sell airtime — a weekly "Game of the Week" time-buy runs low four figures a night and instantly reprices every sponsor asset ("as seen on TV").

LEVEL 4 · YEAR 2–3

ESPN+ / National

High-school hoops reaches ESPN through packagers — Paragon Marketing has curated ESPN's HS slate since the LeBron game in 2002. ESPN airs only ~12 HS games a year, anchored on ESPN-100 recruits. The route in is star power + a broadcast-quality feed, not a check.

Real broadcast cameras. Four ways to get them.

1 · The Belmont play (first call). Belmont's D-I games already stream on ESPN's platforms, produced on campus — meaning camera positions, cabling, and control-room infrastructure live in the building we're renting, and Belmont's Curb College trains sports-broadcast students who need summer reps. Negotiate production access + a student/faculty crew pipeline into the venue deal itself.

2 · Nashville's freelance broadcast bench. This is Music City — the town is full of broadcast-grade camera ops, TDs, and replay operators between tours and TV gigs. A crewed flypack (4–6 broadcast cameras with CCUs, switcher, replay) rents for roughly $5–10K/day — reserve it for championship nights and anything a network might see.

3 · School gear partnerships. MTSU's media college runs one of the biggest broadcast programs in the South with mobile production units; local schools own camera kits that sit idle in June and July. Trade crew credits, stipends, and demo-reel footage for gear and hands.

4 · Own used broadcast glass. If we buy, we buy real: used ENG/system cameras with proper lenses and tripods run $3–8K each — a manned 4-camera kit lands ~$25–50K one-time and becomes a Diamond House asset billable year-round. Operated by real shooters, every night.

Commentator pipeline: Belmont/Lipscomb/MTSU broadcasting programs + local radio talent hungry for reps. Pay $200–400/game, develop your own voices — they become part of the brand like OTE's crew did.

Big-stage feel — cheap, but loud on camera

Walkout tunnel + smoke/light player intros · per-team hype videos on the board · DJ + PA personality · printed court-apron branding · live stat overlays · postgame press-conference backdrop wall (sponsor logos behind every quote) · media-day portraits used in every graphic. None of these cost real money. All of them read "pro" in every clip that leaves the building — and clips are the product.
